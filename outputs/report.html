Two Wheels Ahead

A twelve-month forecast of UK new motorcycle registrations, built from 79 months of MCIA press statistics (scraped from the monthly PDFs) and Met Office weather data — split by style, engine size and brand.

The market, and where it goes next

Registrations run on a hard seasonal clock — March and September plate months, a summer shoulder, a deep winter trough — disrupted by three shocks: the 2020 lockdowns, and the Euro 5+ deadline that pulled roughly a December's worth of extra bikes into December 2024 and hollowed out early 2025. The 2026 season is running +15.6% year on year, and the model expects that recovery to hold.

Seasonality is the engine

The month dummies alone explain most of the variance. March sells ~3× a January; September is the second key — both are new-plate months. Weather, by contrast, barely registers: once seasonality and the shocks are controlled, the only signal that survives is last month's temperature anomaly during riding season (+1 °C ≈ +4% registrations the following month, p ≈ 0.07). Sunshine and rainfall add nothing.

Engine-size mix

Two markets share the plate: small commuters (≤125cc) — a third of volume, strongest in winter months — and big bikes (over 500cc), which own the March and September peaks. The mid class is quietly growing; electric remains under 4% and is drifting down, not up.

Style and brand, next twelve months

Adventure is the biggest style and the fastest-growing (+26% YTD); with scooters and nakeds it covers six in ten registrations. Honda takes one registration in five — double anyone else. Brands outside MCIA's monthly top-ten table are grouped as Other.

Method

The aggregate model is a linear regression on the log of registrations — trend + month dummies + lagged in-season temperature + event dummies (COVID lockdowns; Euro 5+ pre-buy and payback) — chosen because it won the rolling one-step backtest over the last 18 months. A SARIMAX variant with the same regressors came second. Splits are share-of-market models on the 42 months of segment data (the current MCIA taxonomy begins January 2023), renormalised so every split adds back to the aggregate. Intervals are 80%.
